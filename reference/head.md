# The head as a solid

The reason an oval with features on it fails is that an oval has no side plane,
no back, and no thickness at its edge. You are never drawing a contour — you are
drawing the edge of a volume that continues out of sight.

## Two masses and a hinge

Before any feature exists the head is:

- **Cranium** — a large round thin-shelled ball, the top and back of the head.
- **Facial mass** — a smaller *wedge* hung on the front-lower quadrant of that
  ball, carrying the eye sockets, nose and upper jaw.
- **Mandible** — slung underneath from a hinge near the ear. It is its own
  tapered piece, not carved out of the same lump, which is why it opens.

Never merge these into one oval. Decide, in order: the cranial ball's size and
tilt → where the facial wedge attaches and at what angle → the width of the
zygomatic arch (this sets face width, *not* the cranium) → the mandible's hinge
and its gonial angle.

## Bony landmarks and what they do on the surface

| Landmark | What it governs |
|---|---|
| **Brow ridge** | The shelf the eyebrows sit on and the ceiling over the eye; why the upper lid recesses into shadow. The two arches read as a stretched M |
| **Temporal line** | *The* plane break where the side of the skull stops being a wall and becomes a roof |
| **Zygomatic arch** | Face width. It stands clear of the skull with a gap behind it; the cheekbone is this arch pushing the skin out |
| **Orbital socket** | A **rhombus with rounded corners**, not a circle — frontal bone on top, maxilla inner-lower, zygomatic outer-lower. This is why an eye reads diamond-like, inner corner pulled down and pointed, outer corner rounder |
| **Nasal spine / maxilla** | How far the nose root stands off the face; how far the mouth pushes forward |
| **Gonial angle** | The rear corner of the jaw — square vs tapered |
| **Mastoid process** | The knob behind the earlobe the neck's front muscle runs to. Omit it and the neck attaches by no visible mechanism |
| **Occiput** | The back-of-skull bulge. Omitting it is why 3/4 heads look front-heavy and truncated |

## The schema is a diagnostic, never a scaffold

Everything in this section is knowledge about the average head, and the average
head is not the one in front of you. **Do not build on it.** Build on landmarks
measured off the subject, and use what follows only afterwards, to ask why your
measured head differs from the average and whether the difference is the
character or a mistake.

The inversion matters more than it sounds. A head built with a ready-made ball,
a centre line down the middle of it and equal thirds hung off it comes out as a
competent generic head with the subject's dimensions — every feature inside its
correct box, every box within a few pixels, and not a likeness. That is not a
hypothetical: it happened here, and it survived every check in the kit, because
each check compared marks and the marks were all where they were meant to be.
The schema had supplied the form before the subject was ever consulted.

**State the turn before anything else.** How far is the head rotated, and which
way? The answer decides where the centre line goes, and it is not down the
middle of the box: on a three-quarter head the centre line runs down the nose,
close to the far edge of the face. Frontal-by-default is the standing failure —
it produces a face read front-on with a nose stuck to one side of it — so make
the rotation an explicit written decision, place the centre line where the
rotation puts it, and only then hang anything on it.

## Loomis, properly

1. **Cranial sphere** — the brain case only. Draw it **bigger than feels
   natural**. Undersizing it is the commonest error and is what stretches the
   jaw later.
2. **Cross it** with a centre line and a brow line, both drawn as rubber bands
   *wrapped around the ball*, never as straight bisectors of a flat circle.
3. **Cut the side plane** — a flattened patch each side at about **2/3 the ball's
   diameter**, at the **temple**. Putting this cut at the eye is what makes a
   3/4 head read as a flat disk.
4. **Hang the tapered jaw block** below, measured against the sphere's radius.
5. **Four guides**: hairline, brow, nose base, chin.
6. Features last.

### The average head, for comparison only

Not a scaffold, and not "locked" — a set of numbers to hold up against the ones
you measured, so that you can name the departure. Every line below is a fact
about nobody in particular.

- Vertical thirds, **equal**: hairline→brow, brow→nose base, nose base→chin.
- Hairline sits halfway between brow and the top of the sphere.
- Eye line = vertical midpoint of the whole head; eye-line-to-chin = half the
  head height.
- **Five eye-widths across the brow line**: margin, eye, one-eye gap, eye, margin.
- Nose width ≈ distance between the inner eye corners.
- Bottom of the lower lip sits halfway between nose base and chin.
  Mouth slightly wider than the nose, roughly to the inner iris positions.
- Ear top on the brow line, ear bottom on the nose base, behind the jaw.

A specific character is defined by how it *departs* from these — measure the
departure, do not eyeball it, and push it rather than inventing something else.
See `measuring.md`.

### Under turn and tilt

- The sphere is rotated **mentally first**; then every guide is redrawn as the
  curve that results from wrapping a line around the rotated ball.
- **3/4 turn**: the centre line bulges toward the side turning away; the brow
  line curves the opposite way, like an equator on a tilted globe.
- **The far side compresses.** The near half shows more width; the far eye is
  narrower, closer to the centre line, and partly hidden behind the nose bridge
  and the side-plane edge. Getting this backwards makes a face read front-on
  with a nose stuck to one edge.
- **Yaw vs pitch behave differently**: on a horizontal turn the centre line keeps
  its length and slides sideways while the eye line narrows; on a vertical tilt
  the centre line foreshortens while the eye line keeps its width.
- **Ear-base / nose-base rule**: at eye level they sit on one horizontal. Head
  tilted back, the nose base rises above the ear base; tilted forward, it drops
  below. The separation grows with the angle.

## Planes

Four broad planes — **front** (forehead, eyes, nose front, mouth, chin),
**side** (temple through cheek to jaw), **top** (crown), **bottom** (under chin,
nostrils, brow, lower lip — in near-permanent shadow). Every plane edge is a
value break; that is what planes are *for*.

The breaks worth cutting:

1. **Temple/side plane** — anchored by hairline, nose base, temple corner, back
   of skull; visible as the pointy end of the brow turning the corner.
2. **Zygomatic plane** — a strip from the jaw joint forward to the nasal base.
3. **Forehead front-to-side** — S-curves up from the cheekbone's side plane to
   the socket edge, angling in to a **keystone** at the glabella.
4. **Nose** — top, two sides, bottom.
5. **Under the jaw** — the bottom plane.

**The nose-to-chin staircase.** In profile, the front of the face from nose base
to chin is three alternating steps: nose bottom (down) → philtrum (down) → upper
lip edge (up) → lower lip (up) → chin groove (down) → chin boss (forward/up).
Frontally this is an alternating dark/light band, and it is the single thing a
flat line-mouth gets most wrong.

## Features are solids, not marks

### Eye
A **sphere in a socket**, with the lids as **two bands of real thickness wrapped
over it like beanies over a football**. Upper lid = 3 planes, lower = 2. Because
the lids have thickness, the upper shows a shadowed under-plane and the lower a
lit top-plane.

Build: brow-ridge box tilted back → a second box below sloping to the cheekbone →
drop the eyeball in → block the whole socket opening as one shape → group lids +
sclera + iris as one shape to fix placement → only then separate the lid bands.

*Symbol tells:* flat almond outline; lids as a single line with no thickness;
upper and lower lids mirror-symmetric — in reality **the upper lid's peak sits
toward the nose and the lower lid's toward the ear**; iris a perfect circle at
any angle instead of a perspective ellipse; sclera flat white with no gradient.

### Nose
A wedge with **four planes** — top, two sides, bottom — carrying a **ball**
(two cartilage halves hooking under at the septum) and two **wings** curling
under and around.

Build: bridge (bone above, cartilage below, meeting the glabella keystone) →
the major box → the ball with its own top/front/bottom → the wings → minor plane
breaks last.

*Symbol tells:* a 2D silhouette outline; nostrils as two dots with no wing
volume; no tuck-under of septum or wings, so it sits on the face rather than
growing out of it.

### Mouth
Lips are stretched over a **cylinder standing in for the dental arch** — an
inflated tuna can. That curvature is what pushes the lip surface out.

Build: fix the tooth cylinder's tilt and diameter → upper lip as **three
pillows** (central lobe with the philtrum, two side lobes) → lower lip as **two
pillows** with a shadowed pillar between → the **tubercle** (the central wedge
pushing down between them — not a flat cupid's-bow line) → pinch the corners as
bean-shaped nodes, never line-ends → check for overlaps between lip forms, which
is what sells recession.

*Symbol tells:* one curved line with two humps sitting flat on the face; no wrap
around the cylinder; no distinction between the upper lip's downward plane and
the lower lip's upward one — which in real light catch and lose light oppositely.

### Ear
A **megaphone/bowl**. Helix (the rim, a "?" with the lobe as its dot),
antihelix (an interior "Y"), tragus and antitragus (two beads across a U-notch),
concha (the deep central bowl), lobule (soft, about **1/3 of ear height**).

Heavier line on the outer helix (real cartilage bulk), thinner on the interior
folds. On a head tilt the ear rises or falls to keep perspective: higher on a
down-tilt, lower on an up-tilt.

*Symbol tells:* a comma or backwards-6 with no interior; no concha depth, so it
lies flush instead of standing off as a bowl.

## Build order for any angle

1. Decide eye level, and the head's yaw and pitch relative to it.
2. Build the cranial ball / skull box **already rotated** to that angle.
3. Wrap the centre and brow lines around it as curves, never straight lines
   forced to fit.
4. Put the side-plane cut at the **temple**.
5. Fix the tilt of the eye/nose/mouth lines using the ear-base/nose-base rule
   *before* drawing a single feature.
6. Features last, each respecting its own foreshortening: eyes thin toward a
   line in profile; the nose progressively occludes the far eye (how much is a
   direct measure of the turn); lips come *forward off* the face in profile,
   never flattened onto the cheek plane.
